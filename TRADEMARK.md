- Say truthfully that your project, product, or service **uses**, **is built

- Refer to CodeAtlas by name in documentation, articles, talks, comparisons,
  reviews, and academic work — including critical ones.
- Reproduce the logo unmodified when referring to the project, at a size and

- Name a fork, distribution, or derivative "CodeAtlas", or anything

- Use the logo as the icon or primary branding of your own product.
- Use the name in a company name, product name, domain name, package name, or
  social media handle.
- Imply sponsorship, endorsement, affiliation, or official status.
- Sell merchandise bearing the name or logo.

## If you fork

Forks are welcome — that is what the license is for. Please rename. A fork may
accurately state "forked from CodeAtlas" or "based on CodeAtlas"; it may not
present itself *as* CodeAtlas.

Concretely: `my-arch-tool (based on CodeAtlas)` is fine. `CodeAtlas Pro`,
`CodeAtlas Enterprise`, or `Better CodeAtlas` are not.

Please also point the fork's support channels at yourself rather than at this
repository's issue tracker.

## On the attribution in generated output

CodeAtlas stamps `Powered by CodeAtlas — https://codeatlas.live` into the
output it generates: Markdown and Mermaid exports, the diagram canvas, MCP tool
responses, and the agent comment export.

Being straightforward about what this is: **the Apache License permits you to
remove it.** Only the `NOTICE` file is a binding obligation under §4(d). The
attribution is a request and a convention, not a lock, and anyone telling you
otherwise about an Apache-2.0 project is overstating their position.

We ask that you leave it in place. It is how people discover the project, and
it costs you nothing. If you remove it, you still may not use the CodeAtlas
name or logo to describe what you ship — that part is a trademark matter, not a
copyright one, and this policy does apply.

## Changes and enforcement

This policy may be updated. Changes are not retroactive against good-faith use
already made under a previous version.

Enforcement is intended to be proportionate: the purpose is to prevent user
confusion about what is and is not the official project, not to police honest
reference. If you are unsure whether a use is acceptable, ask first — open a
[discussion](https://github.com/vamsikk7/codeatlas/discussions). Most questions
get a yes.
